How it works

Step 1 — label examples

Humans mark thousands of emails as spam or not. This labeled data is the AI's training set.

Step 2 — find patterns

The AI notices which words appear in spam vs. inbox — like "FREE", "!!!", or suspicious sender addresses.

Step 3 — predict

On new unseen emails, the AI applies what it learned to make a prediction — even if it never saw that exact email.

Real-world uses

Medical diagnosis, voice recognition, image classification, house price prediction.

Unsupervised learning — finding hidden patterns
No labels, no teacher. The AI explores raw data and discovers groups by itself — like sorting a box of mixed objects into piles.

Try it: group these animals

The AI has no names — only features like "has fur", "has wings", "swims". Click an animal to select it, then pick a group. Or just reveal how the AI groups them.

How it works

No labels needed

Nobody tells the AI what things are. It receives raw data and must find structure by itself.

Measuring similarity

The AI compares features — fur? wings? fins? Things that share features get grouped together.

No right answer

There's no "correct" grouping. The AI finds whatever natural clusters exist — humans then interpret them.

Real-world uses

Customer segmentation, recommendation engines, anomaly detection, compressing images.

Reinforcement learning — learning by doing
The AI learns through trial and error — getting rewards for good moves, penalties for bad ones. Like training a dog with treats.

How it works

Agent and environment

The AI (agent) takes actions in a world (environment). In this maze, you are the agent.

Rewards and penalties

Good actions earn points, bad ones lose them. The AI runs millions of trials to find the best strategy.

Trial and error

Nobody tells the AI what to do. It tries random moves at first, then learns which ones lead to more rewards.

Real-world uses

Game-playing AIs, self-driving cars, robot movement, energy optimisation in data centres.
